# Epic: research notes

Started 2026-09-09. Research behind the roadmap's epic skill: what an epic is for, what it carries, and how its success is stated. Only the references are written.

## References

Local copies are markdown files in `~/Downloads/books/`, one file per book. Primary first. Each line says what the book has an opinion on here.

- **User Story Mapping**, Jeff Patton. An epic is a slice of the map with an outcome, released by outcome rather than by feature set.
- **Evidence Guided**, Itamar Gilad. Goals to ideas to steps: an epic carries a target outcome, its leading indicators, and a confidence level in the evidence behind it.
- **Empowered**, Marty Cagan and Chris Jones. An epic is a problem handed to a team, not a feature list handed to it.
- **Measure What Matters**, John Doerr. The objective and key results that say what the epic is meant to move.
- **Lean Analytics**, Alistair Croll and Benjamin Yoskovitz. The one metric an epic exists to move at this stage.
- **Continuous Discovery Habits**, Teresa Torres. The opportunity above a set of stories, with the desired outcome above that.

In our other repos and one public one, read 2026-09-09: `~/Development/agentic-temp/summary.md`, the `/epic` section of the earlier suite. Ticket's counterpart over an epic template: summary, goal and driver, area and repo, an owner-tagged breakdown mined from raw material, fields from the project's existing set. Engagement work; confirm provenance before reuse. Matt Pocock's `wayfinder` (github.com/mattpocock/skills, `skills/engineering/wayfinder`) plans work larger than one session as a map of decision tickets on the issue tracker, resolved one at a time.

Considered and not listed: Accelerate, Inspired, Lean UX, The Lean Product Playbook, The Lean Startup, The Product-Minded Engineer, Thinking in Systems. Their opinions land on story or triage. The Design of Everyday Things and The Startup Way are not about these artifacts.

Related in this repo: `story.md` and `triage.md`, the artifacts below and beside this one. `grill-me.md`, examine's wound: a premise the seed gets wrong or a decision it never made, which is most often an epic-level gap surfacing in a story. `orient.md`, how a skill learns what kind of project this is and where its facts live. `brief.md`, the unit of work an epic is broken into.
